# AI Safety Index

A public, buildless static site that shows what is happening in AI safety each week and connects it to the research directions, papers, data/tasks, benchmarks and published results behind it. It serves researchers, developers and people following AI safety. Its first job is to make the week's safety signals legible at a glance; its second is to help visitors find usable resources. Neither a ranking nor a recommendation implies endorsement of research claims, and no page combines results into an overall safety score. Canonical hosting is https://gabrygao.github.io/ai-safety-index/ in `GabryGao/GabryGao.github.io`, under `ai-safety-index/`. Preserve other repository files during publication.

## Structure and audience paths

Approved on 2026-10-29: the site is a set of plain HTML pages sharing one shell (`shell.css`, `shell.js`, `i18n.js`, `catalog.js`). The previous single-page portal is kept unchanged as `legacy.html` for comparison and can be removed once the new site is settled.

- `index.html` — the board: "days since the last AI safety incident" on a green signage panel with a daily incident strip, the latest signals of the last 7 days with inline summaries, a per-organisation table of the last 30 days of safety signals (no grades), and this week's arXiv paper picks. A direction filter rescopes every block.
- `records.html` — every collected signal, grouped by day, filterable by direction, category, source type (official post or incident report), organisation, keyword and incidents only; `?item=` opens one record.
- `directions.html` — overview of the six directions; `?dir=` shows one direction's board, comparison boundary, subtopics with candidate benchmarks, recent signals, benchmarks, papers, tools and datasets.
- Evaluation, four tabs: `results.html` (results board for the five benchmarks with on-site results), `benchmarks.html` (catalog with an evidence ladder and a data-and-tasks section), `methods.html` (tools and runtime environments, comparison boundary, standards and processing steps), `upload.html` (resource form).
- `papers.html` (library by year plus this week's picks), `detail.html?id=` (one catalog entry), `search.html?q=` (site-wide search), `about.html` (team, data sources, link to the previous version).

Old `#route` links are redirected by a small script at the top of `index.html` (for example `#resource/S055` → `detail.html?id=S055`, `#news?content=analysis` → `records.html?kind=analysis`, `#teams` → `about.html`); `tools/verify-site.mjs` checks these.

## Evidence and source policy

The library contains 490 bibliography records: 487 selected from OpenTAI's public GitHub catalog at commit `8910c895c22f90f78205ad45ae0efb3461e63225`, plus three benchmark papers added by hand (now in `site-data.js`). Titles, authors, dates and original links retain attribution in `research-source/opentai-import-provenance.json`. The cached official metadata title match is not independent full-text review or experimental reproduction. Do not imply that inclusion establishes a paper's claims.

Nine historical HELM Safety report records retain model versions and evaluation conditions; they are not a current leaderboard. `research-source/helm-safety-results-provenance.json` records the 2024-11-08 Stanford CRFM source and exact two-setting values, and the results board reads that file directly. Original 0–1 values are multiplied by 100 for display, not reinterpreted as an attack/refusal rate. The results board shares model/developer/configuration filters between its chart, table and CSV export, and only compares rows within one benchmark, task, setting and metric. Preserve original-source links and numeric result tables. No scores come from unverified screenshots.

Decided on 2026-10-29: the site does not use any news aggregator's data, listings or rankings. `tools/update-signals.py` collects safety signals every 3 hours from primary public feeds — official posts from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, the UK AI Security Institute, METR and NIST, kept only when this site's safety rules match — and incident reports listed by the AI Incident Database. Category, direction and organisation are assigned by this site's keyword rules; there is no heat score. Records keep the original title, date, a short publisher summary and the original link, and are kept for 90 days (`signals-feed.js`, `research-source/signals-provenance.json`, which also records per-source health). A failed source keeps its stored records. `tools/update-papers.py` picks 8 papers from the last 7 days of arXiv (cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CR, cs.LG, cs.CV, cs.RO) by this site's safety keyword score (`paper-picks.js`); the picks are automatic and unreviewed, and the pages say so. Both scripts have tests. `news-feed.js` and `topic-feed.js` are no longer refreshed and remain only for `legacy.html`.

Catalog extras that used to live inside the retired UI scripts — hand-written benchmark copy, the 17 evaluation topics, tool descriptions, the three datasets, team entries and the hand-added papers — are now data in `site-data.js`. Edit that file, not `legacy.html`'s scripts.

## Language and interaction contract

The site is Chinese and English. A link in the header switches language and the choice is remembered under `asi-concept-lang`, separate from the previous version's `ai-safety-index-language`. Interface text is written as inline pairs (`T(zh, en)`) or `data-en` attributes; data lookups live in `i18n.js`. Scientific titles, author names, model names, source URLs and identifiers keep their original wording. Long catalog prose (notes, data access, licence) has no English yet: it stays in Chinese and is marked "Chinese only". English written for this site (metrics, subfields, interface) is a draft awaiting review. Japanese and German were dropped on 2026-10-29 and remain only in the previous version. Filters and the selected direction live in the URL. Keep keyboard focus visible and honour reduced motion.

## Visual identity and assets

The world is industrial safety signage, recorded in `DESIGN.md` and `.impeccable/surfaces/index-html.md`: graphite ground, safe-condition green signage panels, white enamel number plates, warning yellow only for incident days and notices, signal red only as a cap for high-heat incidents. Archivo is self-hosted in `fonts/` (condensed 800 for numerals, semi-condensed 700 for headings, 400/600 for interface text) with the system CJK stack for Chinese. The only motion is the plates turning and the strip bars rising on load, both disabled under reduced motion. No raster images are used on the new pages; the previous version's assets stay with `legacy.html`.
